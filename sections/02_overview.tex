\subsection{Background and purpose}
\label{sec:overview-background}

This is the Week~11 reading of a campaign chartered on 7~August~2026. Gilmour Space Technologies flew Eris TestFlight~1 from Bowen in July~2025. The vehicle cleared the tower and was terminated at T+14~s after thrust loss in two first-stage hybrid motors \citep{smith2025,gilmour2025,asa2025}. TestFlight~2 is the late-2026 follow-on, inside the charter's Q1~2027 outer window \citep{dougherty2026}. Group~1 plans and controls launch-site operations, listed in Section~\ref{sec:overview-scope}. Manufacturing, engine research, freight to the gate, and pump redesign remain Gilmour factory work. Orbital insertion is Gilmour's vehicle-success test, not a consultancy finish-line metric.

The Bowen facility licence has been held since March~2024. Stacking (A-123) is a crane lift, mate and bolt of the 23\,m vehicle, with no ignition, so that licence already covers it. Hot fire (A-133) is the five-second static fire of the four first-stage hybrid engines, vehicle still held down. It waits on Australian Space Agency (ASA) flight authorisation, Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA) airspace, and Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority (GBRMPA) marine-park permission \citep{casa2024,gbrmpa2019,asa2025}. Those instruments gate hot fire, not stacking. ASA stays Keep Satisfied, filed 60~days prior on A-113. The CASA Notice to Airmen (NOTAM) is the A1 M-3 airspace instrument and does not replace ASA. The Juru people stay High power / High interest, Manage Closely, now with stop-work authority before Wet Dress Rehearsal. The payload sponsor stays Keep Informed, witnesses LRR on A-141, and has no WBS package.

\subsection{Scope}
\label{sec:overview-scope}

Scope is on the site operations.

\textbf{Included:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item WBS~1.1--1.4: campaign governance and EVM (1.1), pad logistics and GSE (1.2), pad testing (1.3), and flight, launch and closeout (1.4).
\item Seventeen Level-3 packages in Figure~\ref{fig:wbs}; those are the cost accounts.
\item Two extra schedule bars in Table~\ref{tab:precedence} (nineteen bars in total): the split of package 1.2.1 into cleanroom (A-121) and unpacking (A-122), and the external vehicle-receipt wait (A-100).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Excluded:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item Factory manufacture.
\item Engine R\&D.
\item Line-haul to the gate.
\item Payload operations.
\item Any unidentified Level-3 box that would mix factory product into a site baseline.
\end{itemize}

A-100 is a zero-dollar external driver that pins A-122; it is not a consultancy-controlled launch-critical bar. It is omitted from Table~\ref{tab:wbs-cost}. Design defects found on the pad are packaged back to R\&D. Only a pre-kitted spare may be swapped on A-134.

\subsection{Objectives and success criteria}
\label{sec:overview-objectives}

The Week~4 objectives still stand. The network and the budget locked the dates and the dollars. Each test is checked in Sections~\ref{sec:schedule}--\ref{sec:delivery}.

Objective~1 stayed stacking. A1 also put M-4 on 28~October, so one job had two dates and no cold-flow window before hot fire. M-4 is now A-123 on 15~October~2026.

Objective~2 was only ``manage within the allocated budget.'' Crane hire, cryogenic crews and LOX need a figure. The PEP uses a \$1{,}500{,}000 base and \$78{,}890 of residual-EMV-plus-RES contingency, audited at M-4, M-5 and M-7.

Objective~3 was one 30~October output: WDR and the five-second fire together. Hot fire waits for flight permits, so A-132 is cold WDR (16--24~October) and A-133 is the five-second static fire (25--30~October).

Objective~4 stayed pass LRR by 10~November (A-141 / M-6 / HP-4), on the first formal submission. That date was not moved onto lift-off.

Objective~5 stayed the November window and 98.5\% telemetry. A1's M-8 was 22~November, two days past the five-day rule. Lift-off is 15~November (A-143 / M-7) inside the Q1~2027 window, with a more-than-two-day in-flight slip trigger, and handover is 20~November (A-144 / M-8). Closeout on 1~December (A-145 / M-9) is a success test, not a new Objective~6.

Finish-line success, as used in earned value, is final $AC \le \$1{,}578{,}890$ and $T$-0 on 15~November with that $>2$~day trigger. Contingency may be used; nothing above $BAC$ is authorised. HSE stays as chartered: zero lost-time injuries, zero GBRMPA breaches, and zero Juru heritage breaches. Design defects go back to R\&D. Reaching LEO is Gilmour's test, not this consultancy's.
