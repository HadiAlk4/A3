\section{Schedule management}
\label{sec:schedule}

The campaign schedule converts the WBS into a time-phased baseline for board approval. Dates are derived from a finish-to-start activity-on-node network on a seven-day pad calendar from 10~August to 1~December~2026. Approved $T$-0 remains 15~November~2026. A five-day pull-in to 10~November is a contingent response to payload-window or airspace-slot risk; it is not the baseline.

\subsection{Network logic and float analysis}
\label{sec:schedule-network}

Precedence is modelled as activity-on-node with finish-to-start links and zero lag \citep{pmi2021,kerzner2017}. Textbook discrete-time notation writes $EF = ES + D$ when $EF$ is exclusive. On the Bowen calendar every displayed date is inclusive, so the implemented identities are
\begin{equation}
ES_{i} = \max_{j \in P_{i}}(EF_{j}) + 1,
\qquad
EF_{i} = ES_{i} + D_{i} - 1,
\label{eq:fwd}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
LF_{i} = \min_{k \in S_{i}}(LS_{k}) - 1,
\qquad
LS_{i} = LF_{i} - D_{i} + 1,
\label{eq:bwd}
\end{equation}
with $P_{i}$ and $S_{i}$ the predecessor and successor sets of activity $i$, and with $ES$ equal to the campaign start where $P_{i}$ is empty. Total float and free float then follow as
\begin{equation}
TF_{i} = LS_{i} - ES_{i} = LF_{i} - EF_{i},
\qquad
FF_{i} = \min_{k \in S_{i}}(ES_{k}) - EF_{i} - 1,
\label{eq:float}
\end{equation}
taking $FF_{i} = TF_{i}$ when $S_{i}$ is empty. Launch-critical means $TF_{i} = 0$ on a path that sets $T$-0 or closeout. Table~\ref{tab:precedence} is the full pass and matches the locked register.

The forward pass produces two $TF = 0$ chains that remain independent until hot fire. The vehicle path is A-100 (external gate receipt; freight out of scope), then A-122, A-123, A-132 and A-133. The permit path is A-113 into A-133. After A-133 the tail A-134--A-145 is a single critical chain. A-111 finishes on 2~October by an imposed Must-Finish-On (Gate~1) and is not launch-critical: the Week~4 charter already authorised mobilisation.

Path convergence is two staged merges. Wet Dress Rehearsal (A-132) cannot start until stacking (A-123), cryogenic GSE (A-124), avionics power-on (A-131) and Juru monitors (A-114) are complete---the technical merge. Static fire (A-133) then waits on A-132 \emph{and} flight permits (A-113)---the regulatory merge. Launch Readiness Review (A-141) waits on post-fire diagnostics (A-134), not on A-133. Signing a flight-clearance gate on incomplete hot-fire data would break the hold-point logic required of an AS9100D system \citep{sae2016}.

Feeder float is not interchangeable. A-131 has one day into A-132 and is near-critical; A-124 has three. A-125 (RF) has 39 days and is parked in August--September so it does not sit on the October pad peak; moving it does not change 15~November. A-121 is the instructive case: $TF = 3$ but $FF = 0$, so delay is absorbed before $T$-0 only by consuming A-124's float, while A-124's early start moves immediately. Total float protects $T$-0; free float protects the successor. Figure~\ref{fig:network_logic} draws the links. Crimson arrows are the $TF = 0$ launch paths; the navy outline on A-111 is Gate~1.

\subsection{Schedule baseline, Gantt visualisation and milestone integrity}
\label{sec:schedule-baseline}

The dates in Table~\ref{tab:precedence} \emph{are} the schedule baseline. Figure~\ref{fig:gantt_chart} plots the same $ES$/$EF$ values, colours launch-critical bars crimson, and shows remaining total float as a teal whisker to $LF$. Permitting, heritage, mobilisation and GSE run through August and September; October is the physical peak (stack 15~October, WDR 16--24~October, static fire 25--30~October); November is the flight-clearance tail.

Two Week~4 collisions are closed here. The charter dated pad integration 15~October in Objective~1 and stacking as M-4 on 28~October; the PEP locks M-4 to A-123 on \textbf{15~October~2026}, restoring the cold-flow window before hot fire. Handover ``within five days of lift-off'' was also dated M-8 at 22~November (seven days after a 15~November $T$-0); M-8 is now A-144 on \textbf{20~November}. Because the facility licence is already held, A-113 gates static fire, not stacking. Treating flight permits as a stacking predecessor was an initiation error.

Three gates sit on the baseline. Gate~1 is the PEP lock at A-111 on 2~October. Gate~2 is A-113 on 24~October: without those instruments A-133 cannot start, and that path is already $TF = 0$. Gate~3 is A-141 on 10~November. Crashing stacking without crashing A-113 therefore cannot pull $T$-0.

\subsection{Schedule compression: crashing and fast-tracking}
\label{sec:schedule-compression}

\citet{kerzner2017} and \citet{pmi2021} distinguish fast-tracking (overlap of sequential work, usually at zero direct cost) from crashing (added resource on critical work, paid as a cost slope). The Board question is not weather. It is whether $T$-0 can move from 15~November to 10~November if R-12 is realised (payload LEO geometry valid only 10--12~November) or R-14 is realised (CASA NOTAM refuses the 15~November slot). Five days must come off a $TF = 0$ path that actually sets lift-off. After the A-133 merge that path is the tail A-134--A-143. Crashing A-123 by five days, or overlapping A-123 with A-124, saves \textbf{zero} days on $T$-0 while A-113 still finishes on 24~October. A-124 already runs in parallel with stacking.

The crash-cost slope on a crashed activity is
\begin{equation}
\text{Cost slope} = \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta T}
= \frac{\text{crash cost} - \text{normal cost}}{\text{normal duration} - \text{crash duration}}.
\label{eq:slope}
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:compression} and Figure~\ref{fig:compression_curve} compare three options that each claim five days. Only Option~C is both CPM-valid and acceptable against the LRR hold point.

Option~A buys five days at zero direct cost by starting A-141 one day after A-134 starts, so the review board would sit while diagnostics were still open. An LRU swap on day four either rubber-stamps incomplete evidence or recalls the review---a hold-point failure under AS9100D \citep{sae2016}. Option~A is rejected.

Option~B keeps finish-to-start logic but compresses A-134 from six days to one. The slope is $\$40{,}000/5 = \$8{,}000$ per day. A 24-hour diagnostic war room on a vehicle that has just fired is a human-factors problem: thermal and electrical screens are the work most likely to be skipped. The slope is also the dearest of the three. Option~B is rejected.

Option~C splits the five days across two critical activities after the permit merge: three days off A-134 ($\$3{,}000$ per day, $\$9{,}000$) and two days off A-141 ($\$6{,}000$ per day, $\$12{,}000$). The weighted slope is $\$21{,}000/5 = \$4{,}200$ per day, about half of Option~B. Diagnostics remain three days rather than one; LRR remains a finish-to-start successor; A-142 is not crashed, so the four-day range and countdown buffer before $T$-0 is kept. Fatigue is managed with staggered ten-hour shifts. If invoked, the dates become A-134 31~October--2~November, A-141 3--5~November, A-142 6--9~November, and A-143 on \textbf{10~November}. The \$21{,}000 is drawn from the \$225{,}000 contingency only if R-12 or R-14 is realised. It is not loaded into the \$1{,}500{,}000 base, and Figure~\ref{fig:gantt_chart} is not redrawn.

Because Option~C does not shorten A-132 or A-133, feeder floats do not tighten: A-131 stays at $TF = 1$, A-124 at $TF = 3$. No secondary critical path is created. Residual exposure is quality pressure on a shorter diagnostic window, which is why A-142 is left intact and why the option remains contingent.
