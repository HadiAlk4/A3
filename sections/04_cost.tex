\section{Cost management}
\label{sec:cost}

The cost baseline is the WBS in Figure~\ref{fig:wbs} priced at Level~3. Seventeen control accounts sum to a work estimate of \$1{,}500{,}000. Contingency of \$78{,}890 is sized from residual expected monetary value plus four named residual items, not from a round-number markup, so the authorised budget at completion is \$1{,}578{,}890.

\subsection{Estimating basis and assumptions}
\label{sec:cost-basis}

The estimate is built bottom-up at Level~3. Each package is hours $\times$ an hourly rate, plus plant and materials. Four hourly rates are used.

A wage is not what an hour of work costs the project. The employer also pays super, leave and tax on every wage dollar. Some paid hours are never booked to a package. And a crew needs support that is not anyone's wage. The three multipliers below add those costs, one at a time, so each package pays its full share. All four rates start from the same wage and go through the same four steps.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=0.35em]
\item \textbf{Wage.} The Manufacturing Award C10 rate is \$29.45 an hour from 1~July~2026 \citep{fwc2026}. It is the legal minimum for a trade-qualified worker. Engineer and specialist rates start from the same wage so all four rates share one published base. C10 is not the award rate for those two jobs.

\item \textbf{Employment on-cost: what the employer pays on top of the wage.} Every wage dollar carries costs the worker never sees on their payslip. Each one is a share of the wage:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item super 12\% \citep{ato2025};
\item annual leave 7.69\% (4 weeks off in 52, still paid) \citep{fwa2009};
\item leave loading 1.35\% (17.5\% extra on that annual leave, award clause 34.4) \citep{fwc2026};
\item personal leave 3.85\% (10 days in 260) \citep{fwa2009};
\item public holidays 4.23\% (about 11 weekdays in 260) \citep{fwa2009,qholidays1983};
\item long service leave 1.67\% (8.6667 weeks after 10 years, so 0.867 weeks in 52) \citep{qira2016};
\item Queensland payroll tax 4.75\% (employers paying \$6.5 million or less in wages) \citep{qro2024};
\item workers compensation 1.8\% for office staff and 4.5\% for pad staff (assumption).
\end{itemize}
WorkCover Queensland's average premium is 1.343\% of wages in 2026--27, and each industry pays its own rate by risk \citep{workcover2026}. We assume 1.8\% for office work and 4.5\% for pad work, which sits well above that average. The first seven items add to 35.5\%. With office workers compensation the total is 37.3\%, so the multiplier is 1.373. With pad workers compensation the total is 40.0\%, a multiplier of 1.400. For a technician, $\$29.45 \times 1.400 = \$41.23$ is the true cost of one paid hour.

\item \textbf{Utilisation (assumption): paid hours that cannot be charged.} Not every paid hour is spent on a package. Toolbox talks, safety briefings, waiting outside the blast zone and paperwork are paid but belong to no package. Those hours still have to be paid for, so they are spread over the hours that are charged. If 85 of every 100 paid hours are chargeable, each chargeable hour carries the cost of $100/85$ paid hours, which is why we divide by 0.85. The share differs by role. Technicians are on the tools most of the day (85\%). Engineers lose more time to reviews and reporting (72\%). Specialists are on the pad only when their bars are live and wait in between (60\%). These are our planning figures, not measured ones.

\item \textbf{Overhead (assumption): what the crew needs beyond wages.} A crew cannot work on wages alone. Supervision, site facilities, equipment and company running costs are paid by someone, and no single package owns them. The overhead multiplier adds that share to every chargeable hour. It is 1.95 for technicians, 2.67 for engineers, 2.45 for specialists and 1.37 for surge. Surge is lowest because its wage has already been raised by the 1.5 overtime rate. These are our planning figures, set so the four charge-out rates sit where Table~\ref{tab:labour-rates} shows them.
\end{enumerate}

The crane is priced separately: a 100~t crawler at \$4{,}200 per wet day, operator included, from Queensland plant-hire rates checked against Rawlinsons \citep{rawlinsons2025}.

The budget covers the seventeen packages in Figure~\ref{fig:wbs}. Factory manufacture, engine research and freight are out of scope, so A-100 has no cost. The surge crew (\$22{,}400) is already inside WBS~1.3.3. Option~C's \$21{,}000 is not in the \$1{,}500{,}000; it would come out of the \$78{,}890 reserve only if R-14 happens.

Permits and Juru start on 10~August because they take too long to wait. Other August--September pad work is our choice. By Gate~1 (A-111, 2~October), \$602{,}301 of planned work is already under way: 40.2\% of \$1{,}500{,}000. That is planned work, not cash paid. Gate~1 locks the other \$897{,}699 and the \$78{,}890 contingency rules. After that, adding pad work or moving $T$-0 needs a Board variation.

\subsection{Contingency}
\label{sec:cost-contingency}

Contingency is not a flat 15\%. Table~\ref{tab:contingency} adds residual expected monetary value to four named residual items. Treatments already sit in the \$1{,}500{,}000 base (surge hire, dawn-lift window, dual-path recorders, pre-kitted spare), so the pool uses residual $P'_{\%}\times I_{\$}$, not inherent $P_{\%}$. Ten cost-bearing risks sum to \$35{,}760. Itemised RES sums to \$43{,}130. Freight is out of scope and is not in RES. Together they are \$78{,}890 (5.26\% of base), so $BAC$ is \$1{,}578{,}890.

Several register rows stay out of the \$35{,}760 so the money is not counted twice. R-08 (pad-civil defect) is transferred under the design-and-construct package and carries no Gilmour EMV. R-06, R-11, R-15, R-16, R-17 and R-18 are schedule or HSE exposures with no residual dollar draw. R-12 is the TestFlight~1 in-flight mode; the vehicle is factory product, so site-ops EMV is \$0. If R-14 is realised and Option~C is invoked, the \$21{,}000 is drawn from the \$78{,}890 reserve, not added to the \$1{,}500{,}000 baseline. There is no management reserve above $BAC$: unknown unknowns would need a board variation this plan does not authorise.
