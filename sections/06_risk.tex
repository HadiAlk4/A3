\label{sec:risk}

\subsection{Risk approach and scales}
\label{sec:risk-approach}

The process follows ISO~31000 identify--analyse--evaluate--treat--monitor \citep{iso31000}. Expected monetary value is calculated as in \citet{kerzner2017}: money is never read from the 1--5 heat-map scores. Inherent $EMV = P_{\%} \times I_{\$}$ uses the dollar column in Table~\ref{tab:risk-register}. Contingency uses residual $EMV = P'_{\%} \times I_{\$}$ because the named treatments already sit in the \$1{,}500{,}000 base. Probability bands are $P=1$ below 5\% (3\% in EMV), $P=2$ from 5--20\% (12\%), $P=3$ from 21--40\% (30\%), $P=4$ from 41--60\% (50\%), and $P=5$ above 60\% (70\%). Schedule impact $I_{S}$ is 1 ($<2$~d), 2 (2--5~d), 3 (6--10~d), 4 (11--15~d) and 5 ($>15$~d on a $TF=0$ path). Cost impact $I_{C}$ uses a stated dollar scale: 1 ($<\$15$k), 2 (\$15k--\$39{,}999), 3 (\$40k--\$79{,}999), 4 (\$80k--\$149{,}999), 5 (\$150k or more). The matrix coordinate $I$ is the maximum of those two bands \citep{pmi2021}. Residual $P$ falls only where the named response actually bites. Residual $I$ falls only where the effect itself is smaller; a 12--14~day recycle remains $I=4$ even if it is less likely. Ziyad re-scores the register at Gate~1 (2~October), at M-4 (15~October) and at M-5 (30~October), in the same cadence as the EVM dashboard in Section~\ref{sec:delivery-evm}.

\subsection{Top risks, contingency, float and delivery model}
\label{sec:risk-response}

Three rows dominate board attention because they sit on the launch-critical path and can stop $T$-0 or close the heritage case.

R-01 is a residual pump-insulation defect on A-133: auto-abort, a 12--14~day recycle, about \$90k of consumables. Ziyad and the GSE Lead treat it with thermal-soak instrumentation, a pre-kitted LRU spare and a 48-hour swap. Design defects still return to R\&D. The score falls from $(3,4)$ to $(1,4)$: probability drops, but residual impact stays 4 because a recycle is still 12--14~days. Residual EMV is \$2{,}700 ($3\% \times \$90$k), not the inherent \$27{,}000, because the soak, spare and swap already sit in the work baseline.

R-12 is the TestFlight~1 in-flight mode: thrust loss in two first-stage hybrid motors after $T$-0, as at T+14~s, ending the heritage objective. The Launch Director owns HP-3 as the static-fire screen for a ground abort. HP-3 does not treat an event after $T$-0, so residual probability stays $P'=2$. Residual impact stays 5 because an in-flight loss still closes heritage. There is no on-pad redesign and no site-ops dollar draw (EMV \$0).

R-14 is an ASA flight-authorisation or CASA NOTAM refusing the 15~November slot. Abdul files sixty days prior, as the charter scored ASA Keep Satisfied, and holds a weekly ASA/CASA liaison. Crashing A-123 does not treat R-14: permits already finish on 24~October with $TF=0$. Option~C is the schedule backup if this row fires. It is not justified by an invented 10--12~November payload geometry.

R-09 (stage-separation timing) and R-02 (footprint toward the park) remain high-impact register rows on the same LRR protocol. They are not the three board headlines.

Those ten cost-bearing rows sum to \$35{,}760 of residual EMV. Ilsa's itemised RES allowance of \$43{,}130 covers in-scope LOX price variance, crane weather (the only weather dollars), GN2/N2 purge stock and a cryogenic hose/seal kit. Together they are the \$78{,}890 in Table~\ref{tab:contingency} (5.26\% of base, not a 15\% markup). Vehicle freight stays out of RES. R-06 is a dawn-lift schedule control with EMV \$0, so weather is not counted twice.

Float, not money, is the treatment for several feeders. A-125's 39 days let Ilsa park RF in weeks 3--6 so R-13 never meets an occupancy of 14. A-124's three days and A-114's five days absorb GSE and heritage delay before A-132 without moving 15~November. R-08 is transferred after HP-1 NDT; residual $I$ stays 4. If R-14 fires, Abdul takes Option~C to the Board and Ilsa draws \$21{,}000 from contingency. That draw is not in the \$1{,}500{,}000 work baseline, it does not move the M-7 diamond on Figure~\ref{fig:gantt_chart} until the Board says so, and A-142 remains a four-day marine hold.
